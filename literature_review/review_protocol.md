# Review protocol — working version 0.1

## Review design and scope
The current design is a critical integrative review of urban stormwater quantity and quality modelling, centred on EPA SWMM. It covers runoff generation, drainage hydraulics, pollutant buildup/washoff, EMC and first flush, GIS, calibration/validation, uncertainty, and green infrastructure / low-impact development (GI/LID). International studies will be considered, with additional attention to Indian and monsoon-influenced catchments.

This is not yet a completed systematic review. Do not report PRISMA counts or describe the review as systematic until database searches, deduplication, screening, full-text appraisal and exclusion logging have actually been completed.

## Research questions
1. How are runoff quantity and drainage hydraulics represented in SWMM applications?
2. How are pollutant accumulation and washoff parameterised and evaluated?
3. What monitoring design is needed for pollutant loads, EMC and first-flush assessment?
4. Which calibration, validation, sensitivity and uncertainty practices support credible predictions?
5. How do GIS resolution, land cover and drainage connectivity influence model representation?
6. What evidence supports GI/LID scenario assessment, and what are the limitations?
7. What site data are required to apply an integrated framework to a campus catchment in Bhopal?

## Information sources
Search Scopus, Web of Science, Engineering Village, ASCE Library, ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, Wiley, Taylor & Francis, Google Scholar (supplementary citation chasing), and Crossref (metadata/DOI verification). Use EPA SWMM manuals for model documentation, but distinguish technical manuals from peer-reviewed journal evidence.

## Search strings (adapt to each database and retain exact query/date)
- `("SWMM" OR "Storm Water Management Model") AND (stormwater OR "urban runoff") AND (quality OR pollutant* OR contaminant*)`
- `(stormwater OR "urban runoff") AND (buildup OR "build-up" OR washoff OR "wash-off") AND (model* OR simulation)`
- `("SWMM" OR "urban drainage model") AND (calibration OR validation OR sensitivity OR uncertainty)`
- `("urban stormwater" OR "storm runoff") AND ("first flush" OR "event mean concentration" OR EMC)`
- `("SWMM" OR "urban stormwater model") AND (GIS OR geospatial OR "remote sensing" OR imperviousness)`
- `("SWMM" OR "stormwater model") AND ("low impact development" OR LID OR "green infrastructure" OR SuDS)`
- `(SWMM OR "urban stormwater") AND (India OR monsoon OR tropical) AND (quality OR runoff)`

## Eligibility
**Include:** peer-reviewed journal articles and substantive reviews; foundational studies irrespective of age; empirical, analytical or modelling work relevant to urban runoff quantity/quality, SWMM, pollutant processes, monitoring, GIS, calibration or GI/LID.

**Exclude:** unverifiable records, duplicates, non-scholarly commentary, studies with no relevant transferable stormwater process insight, and promotional material. Keep abstract-only papers in a separate pending list; do not describe them as full-text reviewed.

## Screening and extraction
1. Export complete metadata and abstracts.
2. Deduplicate by DOI, then title/author/year.
3. Screen title/abstract against predeclared criteria.
4. Retrieve full texts and record exclusion reasons.
5. Extract citation/DOI, journal, study location, catchment type/scale, rainfall and monitoring design, model/version, constituents, buildup/washoff functions, GIS inputs, calibration/validation, metrics, sensitivity/uncertainty, GI/LID practices, limitations and transferability.
6. Use a second reviewer for a sample or all records where feasible; record disagreements and resolution.

## Quality appraisal
Assess clarity of objectives, adequacy of rainfall/flow/quality data, transparency of model structure and parameter sources, independent validation, uncertainty analysis, reproducibility and whether conclusions are supported. Do not use journal impact factor alone as a quality measure.

## Synthesis plan
Group evidence by: (1) SWMM hydrology/hydraulics; (2) pollutant sources, EMC and first flush; (3) buildup/washoff; (4) calibration, validation and uncertainty; (5) GIS and spatial discretisation; (6) GI/LID; (7) Indian/monsoon applicability. Do not pool numerical metrics unless study designs are comparable.

## Reporting
Use PRISMA 2020 as a reporting guideline if the review is conducted systematically: Page et al. (2021), BMJ 372:n71, DOI 10.1136/bmj.n71. PRISMA is not a substitute for a rigorous search and appraisal.

## Current status
A candidate bibliography of 40 papers has been assembled as a starting corpus. The full-text retrieval, screening log, quality appraisal and detailed extraction remain to be completed. The manuscript must preserve this distinction.
